Chart of Accounts

The list of accounts every entry posts to: heads and postable accounts, types, codes, and shared accounts.

Screen: Chart of Accounts

What it is for

Every amount the ERP records lands on an account in the Chart of Accounts (COA). The COA is a tree: header accounts group others (for example "Current Assets"), and postable accounts receive journal lines (for example "Bank — Main Account").

Before you start

  • You need the Chart of Accounts right for your role (Roles & Permissions).
  • Decide which accounts are for one company and which are shared by all companies (for example a common expense list).

Step by step

Add an account

  1. Open Chart of Accounts and select Add Account.
  2. Choose the Parent Account (the head it sits under). Choose "None" to start a new top-level group.
  3. Enter the Name and choose the Type: Asset, Liability, Equity, Revenue, Cost of Goods Sold or Operating Expense. The normal balance (debit or credit) follows from the type.
  4. Choose Postable if journal lines may post to it; leave it unticked for a header.
  5. Choose the Company, or Shared (All Companies).
  6. For a header, you can set the Cash Flow Category (Operating, Investing, Financing); the accounts beneath it follow it in the Cash Flow Statement.
  7. Select Save Account. The code is given automatically: the next number under the parent.

Find accounts

Filter by company, type, status, or "Postable only". Choosing a company shows its own accounts and the shared ones; "Shared (All Companies)" shows only shared ones.

Move an account to another head

Change its Parent Account and save. The account gets the next code under the new head, only if it has never been used. Once anything has posted to it (or a document refers to it), the move is refused: create a new account under the right head, move the balance with a journal entry, and set the old one Inactive.

Retire an account

Set its Status to Inactive. It stays in the history and reports but can't be used in new entries.

Good to know

  • Some accounts are control accounts, kept by their own screens: each vendor's payables and each customer's receivables account (in the Vendor and Customer masters), and each item's inventory account. Lines on a party's control account must name the party; see Paying and allocating.
  • Cash accounts have codes starting 10, bank accounts 11; Cash and Bank Transactions offers only those as the cash or bank side.
