Paying and allocating against bills

How payments, receipts and adjustments are tied to a vendor or customer and matched to their invoices.

Why it matters

Each vendor and customer has its own account (payables or receivables). To know what is still owed per invoice — for statements, aging and due dates — every amount on those accounts names its party and says what it settles.

The two kinds of allocation

KindMeaningExample
Against RefSettles bills or invoices already posted (or an earlier advance)Paying invoice VI-1021 in full and VI-1034 in part
New RefOpens a new item for the partyAn advance paid before any bill exists

A line's allocations add up to its amount. A line with no allocation chosen is kept as a New Ref for its full amount.

Step by step: a payment against bills

  1. In Cash and Bank Transactions (Bank Payment), add a line on the vendor's payables account and choose the Vendor / Customer.
  2. Select Allocate. The vendor's open items show with their date, due date and Outstanding amount.
  3. Enter the amount against each bill, or select Oldest first to fill from the oldest. The New Ref line under the list shows what is left, kept open for later (for example an advance); All as New Ref settles nothing now. Done keeps it.
  4. Post. The bills show as Paid or Partly paid in the invoice lists.

Settling an advance against a bill

Use a Journal Entry with two lines on the vendor's account: one Against the bill, one Against the advance, same amount on opposite sides.

Fixing a wrong pick: Re-match

A posted payment or receipt that settles the wrong bill (or should have left part as an advance) doesn't need to be reversed. Open the posted entry in Cash and Bank Transactions or Journal Entries and select Re-match references:

  1. Allocate… on the vendor or customer line shows the party's open items, counting this entry's own allocation back in, so the bill it settled now shows its full outstanding again.
  2. Choose again, and select Done: for example All as New Ref, then the right bill; or All as New Ref alone, to make the payment an advance. The New Ref line shows what stays open.
  3. Select Save re-match and give the reason.

Only which items the entry settles changes: its amounts, accounts and date stay, so the books don't change. The bills' outstanding, the statements and the aging follow at once. It works in a closed month too, because nothing is posted. Each re-match is kept with the entry (who, when, the reason, and what it settled before and after). Cancel re-match leaves the entry as it was.

  • Re-match has its own right, rematch, on Journal Entries and on Cash and Bank (Roles & Permissions).
  • An advance (New Ref) that another entry has already set against a bill can't become smaller than what that entry uses; re-match that entry first.
  • Entries posted by other modules (post-dated cheques, returns, retail sales, opening balances) keep their matching in their own module.

Discounts and write-offs

A JV: the party line Against the invoice, and the discount or write-off amount on the expense or income account on the other line.

Good to know

  • A party line may only use that party's own payables or receivables account.
  • When posting, the ERP checks again that each bill still has enough outstanding; if someone else paid it meanwhile, the post is refused with the reason.
  • An invoice can't be reversed while payments are allocated to it: reverse those payments first.
  • Due dates are the invoice date plus the party's payment terms.
  • For a party in a foreign currency, bills, payments and allocations are all in its currency. A bill is settled when its foreign amount is; any difference in the default currency is posted as an exchange gain or loss (Cash and Bank). Cash and Bank, Journal Entries and Post-dated Cheques all take the foreign amount.
