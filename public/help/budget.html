Budget

Each company's budget for its financial year, by account and month, optionally per cost centre or project; approved, revised, and compared with the actual figures.

Screen: Budget

What it is for

A budget is the plan for one company's financial year: how much revenue, cost of sales and each expense you expect, month by month, and, if you wish, how balance-sheet accounts should move (a loan repaid, assets bought). Budget vs Actual then compares the plan with what was posted.

The screen has five tabs: Budgets, Forecasts, Budget vs Actual, Cash forecast and Setup. Choose the company at the top.

Amounts and signs

Amounts are in the base currency. Each account is typed in its natural sign: revenue, liabilities and equity as positive amounts when they grow on the credit side; costs and assets as positive amounts when they grow on the debit side. So a budget reads as people think of it: sales 120,000, rent 24,000. A negative amount means the other way (for example a loan repaid: −2,000 a month).

Step by step

  1. In Budgets, choose the Financial year and select New budget or revision. The year starts as the company's is set in Period Closing → Setup (January or April).
  2. Add lines:
    • Add line: an account and, if you wish, a cost centre and a project. A line without one is the company's: it counts everything of that account not budgeted more finely. An account that doesn't use cost centres or projects can't have them on its line.
    • Start from last year's actuals or last year's budget, changed by a %, and Fill: the lines are replaced. With By cost centre and project, last year's lines keep their cost centres and projects; without it, one line per account. Last year's actuals give the P&L accounts only.
    • Paste from Excel: paste rows into the box (the account code, the cost centre code or blank, the project code or blank, then the twelve months or one year total) and select Add the rows. Rows it can't read are listed.
  3. Type the amounts in the month columns, or a total in the Year column: it is spread evenly over the months, or like last year's actuals when Spread a year total like last year is ticked.
  4. The foot shows revenue, costs and the profit for each month.
  5. Save draft as you go. Submit sends it for approval.

Example: for Example Trading LLC, Rent with cost centre CC1 Workshop, a year total of 12,000 spreads as 1,000 a month.

Approval, the Original and revisions

A budget is approved as Approval Configuration sets for Budget. The amount the levels are judged by is the year's budgeted costs (cost of sales and expenses). With no approval set, Submit approves it at once. The approver sees it in Home tasks, opens it and selects Approve, or Reject with a reason; on a phone, the approval card does the same (Approvals). A rejected budget can be changed and submitted again, or deleted.

The first approved budget of a year is its Original. To change it later in the year, select New budget or revision for the same year: a Revision starts from the Current budget. Once approved, it becomes the Current one; the Original and earlier revisions stay, approved, to compare with. Only one version of a year can be in preparation or waiting for approval at a time.

A project's whole life

Where Setup says projects are budgeted for their whole life too, a project can have a budget of its own, besides its lines in each year's budget:

  1. In Budgets, choose the project under Or a project's whole life and select New budget or revision.
  2. Add the revenue, cost of sales and expense accounts, each with one amount for the project's whole life (no months, no cost centres).
  3. Submit: it is approved, and revised later, as a year's budget is.

In Budget vs Actual, choose the project budget and select Run: each account's life budget, its actual to date (every posted line of the project, whatever the year), what is left and the % used, with revenue, costs and the margin.

Example: project P1 Tower has a life budget of 1,000 for Rent; 400 posted so far leaves 600 (40% used).

Forecasts

A forecast is the year you now expect for the P&L: the actual figures for the months so far, then your estimate of the months ahead. Forecasts need no approval.

  1. In Forecasts, choose the Financial year and the month the Actuals to run to (the month before this one is suggested), then select New forecast. The year needs an approved budget: the months ahead start from its Current version.
  2. The forecast opens on the same grid as a budget. The actual months are shaded and can't be changed. Change the months ahead, or type a total in the Year column: what remains after the actuals is spread over the months ahead.
  3. Save draft as you go, and Finalise when it is done. A final forecast is kept as it is.

Make a new forecast each month or quarter: the list shows each one's revenue, costs and profit for the year, so you can see how the expectation moved. Only one draft forecast of a year at a time.

Example: with actuals to March, Sales of 1,000 a month so far and a year total of 15,000 typed, the nine months ahead get 1,333.33 each (the last month takes the rounding).

Budget vs Actual

Choose an approved Budget (the Current one first), the month the Year to date runs through, a final Forecast to show beside it (the latest is suggested; None for none), and how to Show it: each budget line, by account, by group or by class (the headings of the Chart of Accounts). Select Run.

ColumnWhat it shows
Month budget, Month actualThe chosen month.
YTD budget, YTD actualFrom the year's first month to the chosen month.
YTD variance, %Actual less budget, and as a % of the budget. Green is better than budget (more revenue, less cost), red worse.
Year budgetThe whole year.
Year forecast, Forecast less budgetWith a forecast chosen: the year it expects, and how far that is from the budget (green better, red worse).
  • Lines are grouped as revenue, cost of sales and expenses, with their totals and the Profit, then the balance sheet.
  • An actual with no budget line that fits shows as not budgeted.
  • Select an actual to open its lines in the General Ledger report.
  • Copy copies the table for Excel.

Cash forecast

The company's cash from today: its cash and bank balances now, then the next 13 weeks and each month to the financial year's end, with the money in, the money out and the balance at the end of each. Select Run.

What fills it, as Setup says:

  • Open items for the 13 weeks, then the budget (to start);
  • Open items only;
  • The budget only.
Open itemsWhen they count
Customers' and vendors' open invoicesOn their due date (the invoice date plus the party's payment terms), less credits not applied and cheques already in hand.
Post-dated cheques received or issuedOn the cheque date.
Open Purchase Orders (approved, not yet received)On the vendor's payment terms from the order's date.
PayrollThe last posted payroll's net pay, at each month's end.
Recurring entries with fixed amountsTheir cash and bank lines, on each date due.

The budget counts as cash in the month it is budgeted: revenue in, costs out; with balance-sheet lines as movements, an asset bought is cash out and a loan drawn cash in. A month's amount is shared by its days between the periods.

  • Invoices already overdue are shown above the table and not counted in any week: select a total to see them.
  • Select an amount to see the documents behind it.
  • Copy copies the forecast for Excel.

Example: with 4,000 in the bank, a vendor's bill of 1,000 due in five weeks and payroll of 2,500 at the month's end, the balance falls to 1,500 at the month's end and to 500 in week 5.

The spending check

When a Purchase Order is submitted, or a Vendor Invoice with direct lines is posted, each line is checked against the year's Current budget: the budget line it counts against (an account and cost centre with no budget line counts as a budget of zero), over the period set in Setup, against what is already spent.

  • Spent is the posted figures, and, if the company counts them, open Purchase Orders (approved, the quantity not yet received).
  • Where projects have whole-life budgets, a Vendor Invoice of a project is also checked against what is left of its project's budget.
  • A Purchase Order counts its lines at the vendor's prices; a Vendor Invoice only its direct lines (goods received were counted at the goods receipt). Requisitions have no prices and aren't checked.
  • A year without an approved budget isn't checked.

What happens over budget depends on the company's setting:

SettingOver budget
OffNothing; Budget vs Actual still shows it.
WarnThe message says by how much, line by line. OK goes ahead; the overrun is kept on the document, and its approvers see it (on the document and on the phone's approval card).
BlockRefused. Someone with Budget's override right may go ahead, giving a reason, which is kept with the document.

Example: Rent for cost centre CC1 has 1,000 a month. In March, with 2,700 spent and 200 on open orders, a Purchase Order of 300 is 200 over the year to date (3,000).

Setup

Setup says, for the company, how the spending check works:

  • The spending check: Off, Warn or Block (Warn to start).
  • Checked against the budget of: the document's month, the year to date through its month (to start), or the whole year.
  • Counted as spent: the posted figures only, or the posted figures and open Purchase Orders (to start).
  • The cash flow forecast comes from: open items for the 13 weeks then the budget (to start), open items only, or the budget only.
  • Projects are budgeted: within each year only (a year's line with the project, to start), or also for each project's whole life.

And how balance-sheet lines are budgeted:

  • Monthly movements: the change each month. The budgeted balance is the actual balance when the year began plus the movements, so the plan always starts from reality.
  • Month-end closing balances: the balance expected at each month's end, compared with the actual balance then.

A budget keeps the method it was made with: a change applies to the next budget of a year, and a revision follows its Original.

Rights

In Roles & Permissions, Budget: access reads budgets and Budget vs Actual; write prepares, submits and deletes drafts; setup changes Setup; override goes ahead over budget where the spending check blocks. Approving follows the approval levels.
